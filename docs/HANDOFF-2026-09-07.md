# Handoff: the Leech-tree project

Written 7 September 2026 for whoever continues this work. It is meant to be
read once, in order, before touching anything. It says what is proved, what was
computed, what is published, what failed, and what the one open question now is.

The project's own append-only log is `research_state.md`. It is long and
chronological, and it records every route that failed together with the reason.
This document is the summary; that file is the evidence.

---

## 1. The problem, and where it stands

A **Leech tree** of order `n` is a tree on `n` vertices with positive integer
edge weights whose `C(n,2)` pairwise path weights are exactly `1, 2, ...,
C(n,2)`. Leech (1975) found five, of orders 2, 3, 4, 4 and 6. Taylor (1977)
showed the order must be `k^2` or `k^2+2`. Orders 9, 11 and 16 were excluded by
earlier computer searches, which left 18 as the smallest open order.

**What this project settled.**

* **No Leech tree has order 18** (`Theorem 1.1`). Exhaustive generation of
  forced forests over all 123,867 trees of that order; 59,779,854,336 unique
  nodes; no survivor. The smallest open order is now 25.
* **No spider of order `n >= 5` is a Leech tree** (`Theorem 1.2`). This excludes
  an infinite family and is not an enumeration; a star is the length-one case,
  which recovers Theorem 1.1 of Luo and Yu (2024).
* **Order 25 is reduced, not solved.** Deleting any leaf leaves diameter at
  least 281, unconditionally and by hand. Four certified finite searches raise
  that to 285. Therefore: **no Leech tree of order 25 exists if and only if one
  explicit finite normal form is empty for each of the fourteen values
  `delta = 285, ..., 298`.** Nothing in this project claims order 25 is settled.
* Three neighbouring questions are settled as by-products: `M(11) = 60` and
  `M(12) = 77` for the minimal distinct distance trees of Calhoun, Ferland,
  Lister and Polhill, each with exactly two minimal trees; no modular Leech tree
  of order 9 or 11, while order 5 does admit them, correcting a statement in the
  literature; and exactly six leaf-Leech trees with six leaves.

**A concurrent independent proof of the order-18 result** by M. Ghodsi (Lean 4
reduction to eight first-edge configurations, closed by a separate C++ search,
about 8.6e9 node visits) appeared on 24 August 2026. Our shard-complete zero
result was committed on 18 August and made public on 20 August. The paper cites
this, gives the chronology, and makes no priority claim. The two searches
enumerate different objects and share no comparable intermediate quantity, so
their agreement is an agreement of conclusions and not of counts.

## 2. Why order 25 is stuck

This is the part worth reading twice.

**Every constraint anyone has found is local, and the object has enough freedom
to satisfy all of them at once.**

The only tool that ever excluded a value of `delta` is the crowding inequality
`C(m,2) <= (r+1)(2m-3)`, where `m = 300 - delta` is the length of the block of
consecutive rooted depths and `r = 24 - m`. It fails, and therefore excludes,
only when `m >= 20`. The open range has `m = 300 - delta <= 15` **by
definition**. The instrument that made 281 to 284 tractable is simply absent in
285 to 298.

That is visible in the measured survival of the depth-free pre-filter:

| delta | r | low trees carrying structures | survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| 285 | 9 | 95 of 286 | 33% |
| 286 | 10 | 317 of 719 | 44% |
| 287 | 11 | 1523 of 1842 | 83% |

The filter stops filtering. At `delta = 298` the normal form is a rooted tree on
24 vertices with 22 free low depths and two pinned high vertices: it *contains*
the generic 24-vertex problem.

**The costs therefore run the wrong way.** `delta = 285` was costed at about
1e4 CPU-hours (3,196 of 24,320 (split, shape) pairs were completed, all empty,
before it was stopped). `delta = 286` is about 60 times that. `delta = 287`
alone, at roughly 5e7 CPU-hours, exceeds the whole-problem direct search.

**And the direct search is itself out of reach.** We measured it rather than
guessed: `n = 19` and `n = 20` were run to completion on the cluster
(4.74e11 and 3.88e12 unique nodes, 61 and 510 CPU-hours, no survivors, growth
factors 7.93 and 8.19 per vertex and still rising). Extrapolating gives
1.4e17 to 2.2e17 nodes and 2e7 to 3e7 CPU-hours at `n = 25`.

**What is missing is a global obstruction**: something that looks at the whole
distance multiset `{1, ..., 300}` at once and derives a contradiction, the way
Taylor's parity argument does for inadmissible orders. Every invariant tried so
far constrains a bounded number of distances at a time, and with 7 to 22 free
depths the system has room to satisfy them simultaneously.

## 3. What has been tried and did not work

Do not spend time re-deriving these. Each is either proved and unhelpful, or
measured and unhelpful. `docs/fable-route-review-2026-09-05.md` is the long
form, with the arithmetic.

1. **Moment and generating-function identities.**
   `P(z) = sum_{u<v} z^{d(u,v)}` equals `z + z^2 + ... + z^N` identically.
   Taylor is `P(-1)`; `P(zeta) = 0` at every nontrivial `N`-th root of unity;
   Wiener is `P'(1)`; the signed first moment
   `sum (-1)^{d} d = sum_e w_e q_e (S - q_e) = N/2` is `P'(-1)`; and in general
   `P'(zeta) = -N/(1-zeta)`, with a cut decomposition verified numerically.
   Wired into the depth phase together with Wiener, the signed moment gave a
   **1.01x** speed-up at `delta = 284`: with seven or more free depths the
   two-equation integer system is almost always solvable. `P(i) = 0` yields no
   Taylor-type condition on `n` or on depth-residue class sizes (the star with
   weights 1,2,3,4 and the path 1,1,1,1 share a depth-residue multiset but have
   mod-4 distance counts (2,3,2,3) and (1,4,3,2)).
2. **Sidon-type bounds on the endpoint deficit sets.** Not available. Three
   pendant paths of heights `h, h+1, h+2` at one foot put a 3-term arithmetic
   progression into each of `E` and `F` while all distances stay distinct, so
   distance injectivity bounds neither additive energy. The spine is a Golomb
   ruler and that is already used.
3. **Hall and capacity refinements**: joint interval Hall over unions of
   classes, demand-span, colored moment-Hall with parity resolution,
   partial-domain propagation, a global distance-slot flow. All implemented and
   audited; on a complete candidate they are implied by the exact class sets,
   and the recorded minimal counterprofile at `r = 5` shows total capacity
   cannot improve the bound.
4. **Two-sided crowding.** `g_2 = 300 - diam(T - {a,b}) <= 46` (otherwise
   `T - {a,b}` is a Leech tree of order 23, against Taylor), and above that
   threshold the far graph is a double star. The resulting inequality
   degenerates to the one-sided one and needs `m_a >= 21`, which the open range
   never reaches.
5. **A charge identity.** With charges `+2` and `-3` on the two parity classes,
   `5M + sum_e w_e (2a_e - 3b_e)^2 = 44400`, whose content is
   `sum_e w_e (2a_e - 3b_e)^2 <= 34050`. Under random class placement the
   expected left side is about 11,290, three times below the bound.
6. **The chordal filtration.** For every threshold `t` the graph of pairs at
   distance `<= t` is chordal. A weight-free condition on which pair gets which
   rank; no case found where it excludes anything.
7. **Alternative search orders.** A parameter-free two-anchor search is *worse*,
   not better: a sampling experiment put the part equivalent to the already
   closed case above 1.96e13 nodes. A rooted-depth global search was piloted and
   abandoned at over 1e12 CPU-hours with pruning under 1%. Top-window sumset
   tiling alone is weak (patterns roughly double per level).

**One structural fact worth keeping.** The pair attaining `delta = diam(T-a)`
always contains `b`, so `delta = N - min f`. A consequence: the `delta` ladder
*is* the two-anchor engine's branching on the first `F`-gap. There is no cheaper
collapse hiding in the two-anchor formulation.

## 6. Compute, and the house rules

* **Laptop**: 24 GB, 10 cores, shared with other sessions. At most 4 concurrent
  jobs, `nice -n 15`, cap thread counts explicitly, `pgrep -af` before starting
  and clean up after. Long jobs must be resumable.
* **Cluster**: UCLA Hoffman2 (SLURM, partition `campus`, qos `campus24`,
  23:50 wall, about 100 tasks run concurrently) and a 128-core workstation
  reachable as `geo-ws`. Push anything heavy there.
* SLURM copies the script to spool, so **never** use `cd "$(dirname "$0")"`;
  use an absolute `ROOT=`.
* Linux truncates `comm` to 15 characters, so `pgrep -x <long name>` always
  returns nothing. Use `pgrep -f` with a bracketed pattern, `[f]orest_search`,
  which also stops `pkill -f` from killing the invoking shell.

**Evidence discipline, which this project has held to and which you should
keep.** Label every claim `PROVED`, `FINITE VERIFIED`, `OBSERVED`, `CANDIDATE`,
`OPEN` or `NEGATIVE`. A finite computation over a fixed range is never a proof
of the general statement; it is certified for its exact range and no further.
Every search reports an acceptance token and per-level or per-shard counts that
an independent implementation must reproduce, rather than a bare "no solution
found". Record source hashes with every run. When a re-run agrees with an
earlier one from frozen source, say what that does and does not show: it cannot
detect an error in an abstraction that both runs inherit.

**Mistakes made here, so they are not repeated.** A depth histogram from a
node-limited run was once read as a forced chain and an arithmetic pattern;
both were false and were refuted by a shard test and an exhaustive small case.
A cost comparison was given without measuring one side and was wrong by more
than 4x. A tag was moved twice on the day it was created. And, as above, a
paragraph was deleted from the abstract without checking whether it appeared
anywhere else. The habit that catches all four is the same: measure before
comparing, and grep the whole file before deleting anything that spans sections.

## 7. What we would ask you to attack

In rough order of value.

1. **A global counting obstruction.** Something summing over all 300 distances
   or all 25 vertices that cannot be satisfied, for `n = 25` specifically or for
   `n = k^2` and `k^2+2` in general beyond Taylor. Hypotheses, a proof, and the
   smallest `n` where it is not vacuous.
2. **A different reduction of the whole problem.** Every reduction here comes
   from deleting a leaf. Is there a reformulation as an exact cover, a perfect
   difference family, a question about Sidon sets in a group, a flow, or a
   design, in which order 25 becomes a *different* instance rather than a larger
   one? Different is interesting even if it is not obviously cheaper.
3. **A reason the fourteen values collapse.** Anything making `delta` near 298
   force structure that `delta` near 285 does not, so that the expensive end
   becomes the easy end. At present the cost runs the wrong way.
4. **A well-supported negative.** If your honest conclusion is that no search of
   this shape can decide order 25, say so and show why. We have written several
   such conclusions ourselves and would rather have another than a vague
   positive.

If you want the fully worked version of the question, with the arithmetic and
the closed-case constraint system, read `docs/ASTRA-PROMPT-2026-09-07.md`,
`docs/fable-route-review-2026-09-05.md`, and section 10 of
`theory-lab/double_end/THEORY.md`, in that order.

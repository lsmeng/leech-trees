Dear Editors,

I would like to submit the attached paper, "There is no Leech tree of order 18,
and no Leech spider of order at least five", for consideration in the
Australasian Journal of Combinatorics. It is 35 pages, prepared in LaTeX, and is not under
consideration elsewhere.

A Leech tree of order n is a tree with positive integer edge weights whose
n(n-1)/2 pairwise path weights are exactly 1 to n(n-1)/2. Only five are known,
and Taylor's condition restricts the order to a square or a square plus two,
leaving 18 as the smallest order that was open. The paper proves that no Leech
tree has order 18, so that the smallest open order becomes 25. It also proves
that no spider of order at least five is a Leech tree, which excludes an
infinite family and is not a finite computation, and it proves an unconditional
structural bound at order 25: deleting any leaf leaves diameter at least 281,
which four certified finite searches raise to 285, reducing that order to
fourteen values of one explicit normal form. Three neighbouring questions are
settled along the way, including M(11) = 60 and M(12) = 77 for the minimal
distinct distance trees of Calhoun, Ferland, Lister and Polhill, whose paper
appeared in this journal.

The order-18 result is computer-assisted, and I have tried to make it
falsifiable rather than merely asserted. The search reports eighteen per-level
node counts, which are a mathematically defined sequence, so a referee or a
re-implementer can check the computation level by level instead of taking a
single "no solution found" on trust. Those counts were reproduced by three
runs on two architectures and two compilers, and independently by a clean-room
implementation written from the algorithm description alone. All program
sources, input manifests, raw outputs, certificates and independent checkers
are deposited publicly and are cited in the paper (Zenodo,
doi:10.5281/zenodo.22652936, and the repository tag v1.2-deposit-2026-09-07).

The LaTeX source and any figure files can be supplied on acceptance. I am the
sole author, and I can be reached at this address.
